# Outline an investor pitch deck

Start with the audience, presentation format, and decision the founder wants from this meeting. Ask for the company one-liner, target customer, strongest evidence, and ask if they are missing. For an existing deck, read its actual slide order and any notes before suggesting changes.

## Default sequence

| # | Slide | Investor question to answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intro | What do you do, for whom, and what outcome do they get? |
| 2 | Problem | Who feels the pain, how do they handle it today, and what does it cost them? |
| 3 | Solution | What changes for the customer, and how does your approach make that possible? |
| 4 | Product or demo | Can I see one real workflow, from the starting problem to the result? |
| 5 | Why now | What changed in the market, customer behavior, technology, or regulation that makes this opportunity timely? |
| 6 | Market | Who will buy first, how many of those buyers exist, and how can that initial market expand? Use a sourced, bottom-up estimate where possible. |
| 7 | Competition | What would the buyer use otherwise, including manual work or doing nothing? What trade-off makes your approach win for this customer? |
| 8 | Business model | Who pays, what do they pay for, and how is pricing structured? |
| 9 | Go-to-market | How will you reach and convert the next customers? What have you learned about the channel or sales motion so far? |
| 10 | Traction | What evidence shows demand or progress? Give the timeframe and distinguish paid revenue, pilots, signed intent, and pipeline. |
| 11 | Team | Why are these founders equipped to solve this particular problem and build the business? |
| 12 | Ask and milestones | What action do you want now? If raising, how much, what will it fund, and which milestones should it reach? If seeking introductions, name the buyers or profiles and the milestone the introductions help reach. |

## Adapt the order to the story

- For a ten-slide version, combine solution with product proof and business model with go-to-market. Fold why now into the problem or solution when the timing evidence belongs there.
- Put traction directly after the product if it's the best proof and the audience already understands the market. When the pain needs explaining, give the customer problem and the cost of doing nothing their own slides. When the market estimate depends on how customers pay, explain the business model before market size.
- For a pre-launch company, use the strongest dated evidence available, such as interviews, design partners, or a pilot result. Label evidence, assumptions, and targets at their actual level of certainty.
- Put detailed financials, market math, methodology, and supporting evidence in the appendix. Keep any fact needed to understand a core claim on the slide or in the spoken pitch, depending on the format.

### Worked example — Apparent’s document pitch

Its 12-slide main narrative goes from intro → customer problem → data context → cost of the status quo → solution → platform and service → competitive landscape → business model → market opportunity → traction → team → ask. It spends three slides making the pain and its cost concrete before showing the solution. The business model comes before the market because the pricing assumptions inform the market estimate. The immediate ask was customer introductions tied to a validation milestone.

## Deliver the outline

Return the ordered slides with one takeaway headline, one supporting proof point or demo idea, and any material evidence gap per slide. Write headlines as claims the audience should remember. Use placeholders for missing evidence and flag assumptions. For an existing deck, identify which topics are already covered, where a change in order would help, and which slides could combine.
